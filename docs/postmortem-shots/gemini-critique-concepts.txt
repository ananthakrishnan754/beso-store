The Verdict
Do the 10 feel like variations of the SAME template?

No, they don't look like the same template, but they do suffer from the same creative anxiety. They alternate between wanting to be high-art (brutalist, massive type) and wanting to be functional eCommerce (stats, tiny specs, CTAs). The typography and grid patterns are wildly inconsistent across them all.

Do ANY look like premium brand quality (Vitra / Herman Miller / Hem / Muuto)?

No. Brands like Vitra, Herman Miller, and Muuto rely heavily on narrative and materiality. Their sites feature large, natural photography with warmth and authentic human context, or highly disciplined, sculptural studio shots with generous, natural padding and razor-sharp type. None of these layouts have the confidence to let the materials, textures, and craftsmanship breathe. Instead, they try to compensate with over-stylized typography (Concept 3, 7, 10) or lazy, empty layouts (Concept 1, 4).

The Offenders

The Single Worst Offender: Concept 10. It goes completely off the rails for a luxury brand. The chunky, red uppercase text, crowded badge rows, and cheap copywriting ("NO JOKES.") transform the brand from high-end furniture to a budget direct-to-consumer office chair hustle.

The Single Best (or "Least Worst") Offender: Concept 2. While still quite generic, it's the only one that approaches the necessary visual balance. The split-screen works, the product is front and center (though it needs better lighting), and the typography—while still needing refinement—retains a structured hierarchy that doesn't scream for attention. With tighter alignment and a natural environment for the desk, this could pass.
